{\rmfamily\itshape\fontsize{50}{56}\selectfont\color{accent} Lakȟótiyapi}\\[2pt]
{\fontsize{18}{22}\selectfont\color{midgray}\emph{the language under test}}
\vspace{14pt}

\begin{block}{Why test this?}
\lak{Lakȟótiyapi} (Lakota) is a Siouan language UNESCO classifies as \textbf{critically endangered} --- fewer than 2,000 first-language speakers, most over 65. LLMs advertise multilingual translation, but coverage is undisclosed. For endangered languages with scarce digitized text, the assumption that a supported language ``works'' is \textbf{untestable without direct evaluation}.
\end{block}

\begin{block}{Setup}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{7 models.} Proprietary: Gemini 3.1 Pro, Claude Opus 4.6, Claude Sonnet 4.6, GPT-5.2. Open-weight: DeepSeek-V4-Pro, Qwen3.6-Plus, GLM-5.1.
\item \textbf{200 sentence pairs} from the New Lakota Dictionary, conversational register, \textbf{both directions}.
\item \textbf{Two conditions:} baseline vs.\ extended reasoning.
\item \textbf{Metrics:} chrF++ + BLEU; diacritic-normalized chrF++; an LLM \emph{semantic} judge.
\end{itemize}
\end{block}

\begin{block}{Standard Lakota Orthography}
SLO marks phonemic distinctions with diacritics that models often \emph{drop or rearrange}:
\smallskip
\begin{itemize}
\item \lak{ȟ} (caron) vs.\ \lak{h} --- distinct consonants
\item \lak{ą} \lak{į} \lak{ų} (ogonek) --- nasalized vowels
\item \lak{á} (acute) --- stress\;\;\textperiodcentered\;\;\lak{ŋ} --- velar nasal\;\;\textperiodcentered\;\;\lak{ʼ} --- glottal stop
\end{itemize}
\smallskip
Stripping the marks adds \textbf{+3.3 to +6.5 chrF++} on E\,$\rightarrow$\,L --- isolating orthographic from lexical error.
\end{block}

\begin{column}{0.31\paperwidth}

\begin{block}{Why two judges?}
chrF++ rewards surface overlap; it cannot distinguish fluent-but-wrong from correct. We judge \emph{meaning} with an LLM, English-to-English (no Lakota knowledge required). To rule out family-level self-judging bias, every pair is judged by \textbf{two models from different families} --- Gemini 3 Flash and the independent Llama-3.3-70B --- which agree substantially: Cohen's $\kappa=0.75$ (0.89 quadratic-weighted).
\end{block}

\begin{block}{Reasoning buys calibration, not capability}
For the open-weight models, extended reasoning leaves translation quality and meaning \emph{flat} but raises \textbf{refusals}:
\smallskip
\begin{itemize}
\item Qwen3.6-Plus, E\,$\rightarrow$\,L: \textbf{0\,$\rightarrow$\,36} refusals (of 200).
\item GLM-5.1, L\,$\rightarrow$\,E: \textbf{1\,$\rightarrow$\,16}.
\end{itemize}
\smallskip
Reasoning surfaces the limitation rather than overcoming it. The binding constraint is \textbf{knowledge, not inference}.
\end{block}

\begin{block}{Takeaways}
\begin{itemize}
\item No frontier LLM reliably translates Lakota in either direction (as of 2026).
\item \textbf{chrF++ overstates} low-resource capability --- pair it with an LLM semantic judge.
\item Open-weight models do not close the gap; reasoning changes \emph{refusals}, not quality.
\end{itemize}
\end{block}
